Avellaneda–Stoikov market making on intraday replay

How should a market maker move its quotes as inventory builds up, and does inventory-aware quoting actually control risk on real intraday price paths?

Python, stochastic control, EODHD intraday data, 2026. Code on GitHub

The question

The Avellaneda–Stoikov model shifts a market maker's reservation price against its inventory: long inventory pushes quotes down to encourage selling, short inventory pushes them up. The project starts from a pure Brownian simulation and replaces the simulated path with real one-minute bars, to see how the idea behaves on actual prices.

Approach

  • Data. One-minute regular-session bars for AAPL, MSFT, and NVDA on 29 May 2026, with the bar close as a mid-price proxy.
  • Calibration. Starting price, bar interval, and volatility come from each ticker's path; risk aversion, fill-intensity parameters, and the inventory limit are held as controlled assumptions, because bars cannot reveal true order arrival rates.
  • Fills. Both strategies share the same fill model, λ(δ) = A·e−kδ: quotes closer to mid fill more often.
  • Benchmark. A naive market maker with a fixed half-spread that never adjusts for inventory.

The fixed-spread quoter earned more raw P&L on all three names, but it did so by carrying two to eight times as much inventory. On MSFT, which rose about 3% during the day, much of the naive strategy's profit was directional exposure it never meant to take. NVDA is the honest counterexample: there the inventory-aware strategy was profitable but had the larger drawdown, so the risk-control edge is not automatic.

What I learned

Market making should not be judged on final P&L alone. A strategy that earns more by holding a larger position is taking a different risk, not doing the same job better.

Next

More sessions and averaged results, parameter sweeps over risk aversion and fill intensity, fees and rebates, and real bid/ask or order-book data so fill rates can be estimated rather than assumed.
